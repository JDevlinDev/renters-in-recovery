Illinois tenant alert

Recovery housing is still housing.

If you pay to live in a sober living home, you are almost certainly a tenant under Illinois law. A "guest agreement" can't sign your rights away, and no house manager can put you on the street the same day.

Know your rights Report an operator

Is your house doing this?

  • Contracts waiving your renters' rights
  • Threats of same-day eviction
  • Rent disguised as "donations"
  • Dangerous overcrowding
  • Unpermitted construction
  • Faulty or ungrounded wiring
  • Missing smoke detectors
  • Unlicensed staff acting as counselors

Any one of these may be illegal.

Know your rights

Your rights don't stop at the front door

Licensed treatment facilities play by different rules, and that's exactly why some housing operators pretend to be one. If the place you pay to live isn't a licensed facility, these protections apply to you.

  • 01

    Paying for a room makes you a tenant

    Calling rent a "donation," "program fee" or "house dues" doesn't change what it is. Neither does calling you a "guest," "client" or "resident."

    Illinois Eviction Act, 735 ILCS 5/9

  • 02

    Only a judge can evict you

    Changing the door code, packing up your things, or telling you to be gone "today" without a court order is an illegal lockout. In Cook County, only the Sheriff enforces an eviction order.

    735 ILCS 5/9 · Chicago RLTO § 5-12-160

  • 03

    A waiver can't erase your rights

    A form saying you "do not have renters' rights" doesn't override state law. In Chicago and suburban Cook County, rental terms that waive tenant protections are unenforceable.

    Chicago RLTO § 5-12-140 · Cook County RTLO

  • 04

    Breaking a house rule still means court

    Even if an operator says you broke a rule, they must give written notice and win an eviction case. A rule violation is never a license for a same-day lockout.

    735 ILCS 5/9-210

  • 05

    Reporting problems is protected

    A landlord can't threaten, harass or evict you for reporting code violations to the city, or for talking with other tenants about your rights.

    Retaliatory Eviction Act, 765 ILCS 720 · RLTO § 5-12-150

  • 06

    Recovery is protected by fair housing law

    People in recovery from a substance use disorder are protected from housing discrimination as people with a disability, at both the federal and state level.

    Fair Housing Act · Illinois Human Rights Act

Red flags

The same-day eviction playbook

Some operators borrow the language of recovery to run unlicensed rooming houses outside the law. If your house does these things, you're not alone, and it's worth talking to legal aid.

  • Rent called a "donation"

    Mandatory weekly payments are booked as voluntary charitable contributions.

  • "Guests," not tenants

    You're made to sign a form saying you have no renters' rights and must leave "that same day" if asked.

  • Counselors without a license

    House staff demand on-the-spot drug tests, lecture you about "the program," and threaten to "document a refusal."

  • Telling police it's a "program"

    Officers are told you broke treatment rules, so an illegal lockout looks like routine discharge.

  • Faith or meetings under threat

    You must hit meeting quotas or attend religious activities, or lose your bed.

  • Packed, unsafe buildings

    Dozens of people in a small apartment building, with unpermitted partitions, missing smoke detectors and ungrounded wiring.

  • Retaliation after inspections

    Sudden "rule violations" or threats the day after city inspectors visit.

  • Money in circles

    A nonprofit or LLC pays "rent" to properties owned by the same people who run it.

What the records show

Complaints filed

Normandy House, Inc.

Des Plaines and Chicago · Complaints filed with the IRS, the Illinois Attorney General and the City of Des Plaines

  • $551,024 in 2025 resident payments reported as "contributions," according to the IRS complaint
  • $366,000 paid out as "rent expense" in 2025, which the complaint says went to properties tied to the organization's president
  • $2.4M+ in "contributions" reported since 2021, which the complaint calls misclassified rent
  • Residents sign written rules calling them "guests" who "do not have renters' rights." Anyone asked to leave must "leave that same day."
  • The IRS complaint alleges that mandatory weekly fees are labeled donations, and that the organization answered "No" to related-party transactions on its Form 990.
  • Reports to Des Plaines describe a building assessed as four units housing roughly three dozen people, with missing smoke detectors and ungrounded outlets. The city routed requests #4015–#4020 to Fire Prevention and building inspectors.
  • A civil rights complaint to the Attorney General alleges residents are pushed into faith-based activities under threat of immediate eviction.
  • The complainant reports that the day after city inspectors visited, a house manager entered his unit unannounced, demanded an immediate drug test, and threatened to remove him that day.

Civil complaint

Stairway to Freedom LLC & Phoebe 649 Investments LLC

Chicago · Verified complaint, Sherrick v. Gore et al., Circuit Court of Cook County

  • The complaint alleges that on March 26, 2026, one day after the tenant sent a written cease-and-desist letter, staff changed the electronic door code and locked him out in freezing weather, without any court order.
  • It alleges staff told Chicago police the removal was for breaking the rules of a treatment program, and that the exchange is on police body-camera footage.
  • It alleges the tenant was rented a basement unit where groundwater leaked into the bedroom through a failing foundation.
  • It alleges that after a staff member hit another resident, the program director texted the tenant to "keep this to yourself."
  • It alleges that once the tenant prepared to sue, the owner called and threatened a counterclaim accusing him of taking $25,000 from a safe.

Free legal help

You don't have to figure this out alone. These organizations help Illinois renters for free.

Locked out or threatened right now?

  1. Stay calm. Don't force your way in or get into a confrontation.
  2. Call the police and say: "This is an illegal lockout. I pay rent here. It's a civil landlord-tenant matter." Leave "program" out of it.
  3. Show proof you pay to live there: receipts, Zelle, Venmo or Cash App history, and mail sent to the address.
  4. Call a tenant hotline or legal aid the same day. Numbers are below.

Recording? Do it openly and say so out loud. Illinois law requires consent to record private conversations.
